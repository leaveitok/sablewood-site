Pay your water bill

Ways to pay your City of Sablewood utility account, billing dates, and what happens when a payment is late.

Ways to pay

The City of Sablewood bills water, wastewater and solid waste on a single monthly utility statement. You may pay any of the following ways.

  • Online. Pay by bank draft or card through the resident portal at any time. Card payments carry a $2.15 vendor convenience fee; bank drafts are free.
  • By phone. Call the automated payment line at (940) 555-0111, available 24 hours a day. You will need your 10-digit account number.
  • In person. At the Utility Billing counter, City Hall, 100 Civic Plaza, Monday–Friday, 8:00 a.m. – 5:00 p.m.
  • Drop box. A 24-hour drop box is located in the City Hall north parking lot. Do not place cash in the drop box.
  • By mail. City of Sablewood Utility Billing, P.O. Box 4120, Sablewood, TX 76077.

Billing and due dates

Statements are mailed on the first business day of each month and are due on the 16th. If the 16th falls on a weekend or a city holiday, the due date moves to the next business day.

Payment arrangements

If you cannot pay in full, contact Utility Billing before your due date. Staff can set up a deferred payment agreement spreading a past-due balance over up to three billing cycles. An account with an active arrangement in good standing is not disconnected.

Medical needs. If someone in your household depends on electrically powered medical equipment, file a Critical Care affidavit with Utility Billing. It does not waive the bill, but it changes how and when service may be interrupted.

Starting or stopping service

New service requires a completed application, a government-issued photo ID and a deposit of $75.00 for an owner-occupied residence or $150.00 for a rental. Applications received before 3:00 p.m. are normally connected the next business day.

To stop service, give at least two business days' notice and provide a forwarding address. Your final bill is issued within 15 days and any deposit is applied to it.

Understanding a high bill

The most common cause of a sudden increase is an irrigation system running on a controller that was never adjusted for the season, followed by a leaking toilet flapper. Utility Billing can pull 60 days of hourly consumption for your meter at no charge, which will usually show whether the usage is continuous — the signature of a leak — or clustered at specific hours.
