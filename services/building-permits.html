Building permits and inspections

When a permit is required, current fees, how plan review works, and how to schedule an inspection in the City of Sablewood.

When you need a permit

A permit is required before you begin work on any of the following:

  • New construction, additions, and any change to a structure's footprint
  • Interior remodeling that moves or removes a wall, or alters plumbing, mechanical or electrical systems
  • Roof replacement, including overlays
  • Water heaters, HVAC replacement, and re-piping
  • Fences over 6 feet, retaining walls over 4 feet, and any fence in a front yard
  • Swimming pools, spas, and the barriers required around them
  • Accessory structures over 120 square feet
  • Driveway approaches and any work in the public right-of-way

No permit is required for painting, floor covering, cabinet replacement, or a storage building of 120 square feet or less that is not on a permanent foundation and has no plumbing or electrical service.

Plan review

Residential plan review takes five to seven business days from a complete submittal. Commercial review takes 10 to 15 business days and includes fire and engineering review. The clock starts when the submittal is complete — an application missing a site plan or a signed contractor registration is not in the queue, and this is the single most common reason a permit takes longer than a resident expects.

Contractor registration

Every contractor pulling a permit must be registered with the city and carry general liability coverage of at least $300,000. Registration runs $110 annually and expires December 31 regardless of when it was issued. Electrical, plumbing and mechanical contractors must also provide a current state license.

Scheduling an inspection

Request inspections through the permit portal or by calling (940) 555-0131 before 3:00 p.m. for next-business-day service. Same-day inspections are not offered. Someone over 18 must provide access, and the approved plans and the permit card must be on site — an inspector who cannot reach the work records a failed inspection and the re-inspection fee applies.

Do not cover work before it is inspected. Framing, rough plumbing, rough electrical and mechanical rough-in must all be inspected while still exposed. Covered work will be ordered opened at the permit holder's expense.

Expiration

A permit expires if work has not started within 180 days of issuance, or if work is abandoned for 180 days. One 90-day extension may be requested in writing before expiration.
