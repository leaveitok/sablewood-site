Report a code violation

What the city enforces, how to file a complaint, how long the process takes, and what happens if a property is not brought into compliance.

What we enforce

Code Compliance responds to violations of the city's property maintenance, nuisance and zoning ordinances. The most frequent are:

  • High grass and weeds exceeding 12 inches on any developed lot
  • Junk, trash and debris visible from a public street or an adjoining lot
  • Junked vehicles — inoperable, unregistered or wrecked, and visible from a public place
  • Illegal signage, including signs in the right-of-way and portable signs without a permit
  • Substandard structures that are open, unsecured or structurally unsound
  • Prohibited outdoor storage in a residential front or side yard
  • Work without a permit, referred jointly with Development Services

Some matters are not code violations and are handled elsewhere: barking dogs and loose animals go to Animal Services, parking on a public street goes to the Police Department, and disputes over a fence exactly on a property line are civil matters between owners.

How to file a complaint

Report online, by phone at (940) 555-0150, or in person at City Hall. Give the address and what you observed. A complaint may be filed anonymously, though an anonymous complaint means we cannot come back to you for detail, and a case that turns on a detail nobody can confirm is usually closed.

Your name is not released to the property owner by the city. Complaint records are, however, government records and may be subject to release under the Texas Public Information Act.

What happens next

  1. Inspection — an officer inspects within three business days of the report.
  2. Notice — if a violation exists, the owner receives written notice with a compliance deadline: 10 days for grass and weeds, 30 days for most structural items.
  3. Re-inspection — the officer returns after the deadline.
  4. Abatement or citation — for grass and weeds the city may mow and place the cost plus a $100 administrative fee as a lien against the property. Other violations are cited to municipal court, where each day of continued violation is a separate offense carrying a fine of up to $500, or up to $2,000 for a violation affecting public health and safety.

How long it takes

A straightforward grass case closes in about three weeks: three days to inspect, ten days' notice, and a re-inspection. Structural cases take considerably longer because the notice period is longer and the owner has a right to be heard before a building standards panel.
